\begin{question}

\[ G = \{ a + b\sqrt{-5} : a,b \in \mathbb{Z} \} \]
and \( \boldsymbol{+}_{G} \) be
\[ (a + a^\prime) + (b + b^\prime)\sqrt{-5} \]
and a subgroup of \( G \)
\[ H = \{ a + b\sqrt{-5} : a,b \in 4\mathbb{Z} \} \]

\vspace{1cm}

\qpart

\qsubpart

The identity element, \( e \), of \( G \) is, \( 0 + 0\sqrt{-5} \).

\qsubpart

An element of \( H \) that is not the identity is, \( 4 + 8\sqrt{-5} \).

\qsubpart

An element of \( G \) that is not in \( H \) is, \( 3 + 5\sqrt{-5} \).

\qsubpart

The sum, under \( \boldsymbol{+}_{G} \), of these two elements are,
\begin{align*}
(4 + 8\sqrt{-5}) + (3 + 5\sqrt{-5}) &= (4 + 3) + (8 + 5)\sqrt{-5}\\
&= 7 + 13\sqrt{-5}
\end{align*}

\vspace{3cm}

\qpart

\textbf{G1 Closure}:

Let,
\[ x = a + b\sqrt{-5} \quad \text{ and } y = a^\prime + b^\prime\sqrt{-5} \]

then
\[ x + y = (a + a^\prime) + (b + b^\prime)\sqrt{-5} \]

and as \( \mathbb{Z} \) is closed under addition \( a + a^\prime \in \mathbb{Z} \text{ and } b + b^\prime \in \mathbb{Z}\),
Hence, \( x + y \in G \).

\vspace{2cm}

\textbf{G2 Identity element}:

Let,
\[ e = 0 + 0\sqrt{-5} \]
and
\[ x = a + b\sqrt{-5} \]
then
\begin{align*}
x + e &= (a + 0) + (b + 0)\sqrt{-5}\\
&= a + b\sqrt{-5}\\
&= x
\end{align*}

\vspace{2cm}

\textbf{G3 Inverse element}:

Let,
\[ x = a + b\sqrt{-5} \]
then
\begin{align*}
x + (-x) &= (a + -a) + (b + -b)\sqrt{-5}\\
&= 0 + 0\sqrt{-5}\\
&= e
\end{align*}

\vspace{2cm}
\textbf{G4 Associativity}:

The associativity condition holds for addition in \( \Z \),

Hence \( (G, \boldsymbol{+}_{G}) \) is a group.

\clearpage

\qpart

Using \textup{Proposition 2.5; Subgroup criteria, HB Chapter 5 p26.}

\[ x,y \in H \implies x - y \in H \]

Let,
\[ x = 4m + 4n\sqrt{-5} \quad \text{ and } y = 4p + 4q\sqrt{-5} \]

then
\begin{align*}
x - y &= (4m - 4p) + (4n - 4q)\sqrt{-5}\\
&= 4(m - p) + 4(n - q)\sqrt{-5}
\end{align*}

As \( m - p \in \mathbb{Z} \text{ and } n - q \in \mathbb{Z} \), then \( x - y \in H \).
Hence, \( H \) is a subgroup of \( G \).

\vspace{3cm}

\qpart

The number of distinct cosets of \( H \) in \( G \) is \( 16 \).

\begin{itemize}
\item \( a \pmod{4} \) has 4 possible values; \( 0,1,2,3 \) in \( 4\Z \).
\item \( b \pmod{4} \) has 4 possible values; \( 0,1,2,3 \) in \( 4\Z \).
\end{itemize}

Thus, by the counting principle, the total number of cosets is \( 4 \times 4 = 16 \).

\vspace{1cm}

\qpart

The order of the element \( 1 +\sqrt{-5} + H \in \frac{G}{H} \) is 4.

\[ \left(1 + \sqrt{-5}\right) + H, \quad 2\left(1+\sqrt{-5}\right) + H, \quad 3\left(1+\sqrt{-5}\right) + H, \quad 4\left(1+\sqrt{-5}\right) + H  = H \]

\end{question}

\qpart

Let \( H = \{ r^{2i}s^{j}:i=0,\dots,24, j=0,1 \} \)
    
Using \textup{Proposition 2.4, HB p25}.

\begin{proof}
$ $\newline

\textbf{Non-empty:}

Let \( i=0, j=0 \)
\[ r^{0}s^{0} = e \in H \]
Thus, H is non-empty.

\vspace{2cm}

\textbf{Closure }\( x,y \in H \implies x^{-1}y \in H: \)

Let
\[ x = r^{2a}s^{b} \text{ and } y = r^{2c}s^{d}, \quad a,c\in\{ 0,\ldots,24 \}, \quad b,d\in\{0,1\}  \]
then
\begin{align*}
x^{-1} &= s^{b}r^{-2a}\\[8pt]
\snote{Since \( sr = r^{-1}s \), we can move \( s \) past powers of \( r \).}\\
x^{-1}y &= r^{-2a}s^{b} \cdot r^{2c}s^{d}\\[8pt]
&= r^{-2a} \cdot s^{b+d} \cdot r^{2c}\\[8pt]
\end{align*}

Let \( j = 0 \),
\begin{align*}
x^{-1}y &= r^{-2a} \cdot r^{2c}\\[8pt]
&= r^{2(c - a)} \in H
\end{align*}

Let \( j = 1 \),
\begin{align*}
x^{-1}y &= r^{-2a}s \cdot r^{2c}s\\[8pt]
\snote{Again since \( sr = r^{-1}s \), we can move \( s \) past powers of \( r \).}\\
&= r^{-2a} \cdot r^{2c} \cdot s^{2}\\[8pt]
\stext{As \( s^{2} = e \), }
&= r^{2(c - a)} \in H
\end{align*}

Hence, \( H \) is a subgroup of \( D_{50} \).

\end{proof}

\vspace{3cm}

\qpart

The order of the group \( D_{50} \) is \( 100 \).
The order of the subgroup \( H \) is \( 50 \).

Hence the index of H in \( D_{50} \) is 
\[ \frac{\mid D_{50} \mid}{\mid H \mid} = \frac{100}{50} = 2 \]

As the index of \( H \) is \( 2 \), its left and right cosets are the same, hence \( H \) 
is a normal subgroup of \( D_{50} \), by \textup{Proposition 3.7, HB Chapter 6, p27.}

\vspace{2cm}

\qpart

Let \( k \) be the subgroup generated by \( r^{25} \).

The order of \( k \) is \( 2 \), as
\begin{align*}
(r^{25})^{1} &= r^{25}\\
(r^{25})^{2} &= r^{50} = e
\end{align*}

Which is isomorphic to \( \Z_{2} \) which also has order \( 2 \).

\vspace{2cm}

\qpart

Given \( D_{50} \langle sr = r^{49}s = r^{-1}s \rangle \) ;

\[ Z(H) = \{\,a \in H : ah = ha \ \forall\, h \in H \} \]

\begin{align*}
sr^{n} &= r^{-n}s\\[8pt]
sr^{25} &= r^{-25}s\\[8pt]
&= r^{50-25}s\\[8pt]
&= r^{25}s
\end{align*}

Since \( sr^{25} = r^{-25}s = r^{25}s \) (because \( r^{50} = e \)), \( r^{25} \) commutes with both
\( r \) and \( s \) and so lies in the centre. 
By \textup{Exercise 2.5 of chapter 7,} subgroups of \( Z(G) \) are normal subgroups of 
\( G \), thus \( K \) is normal.

Hence, by \textup{Proposition 3.7, HB chapter 6 p27}, \( k \) is a normal subgroup of \( D_{50} \).

\vspace{2cm}

\qpart

All elements in the subgroup \( H \) are of the form \( r^{2i}s^j \), with the exponent of the \( r \) 
term being even and the element \( e \).

As \( r^{25} \) has an odd exponent, its only other element is \( e \).

Thus \( H \cap k = \{ e \} \).

\vspace{2cm}

\qpart

By \textup{Proposition 1.9, HB Chapter 6 p31}, 

\[ D_{50} \text{ has order } 100 \]
\[ H \text{ has order } 50 \]
\[ k \text{ has order } 2 \]

As \( 100 = 50 \times 2 \), and \( H \cap k = \{ e \} \),
then \( D_{50} \cong H \times k \).

\end{question}

\begin{question}

Given \( G \) is a group of order \( 48668 = 4 \times 23^{3} \).

\qpart

By \textup{Definition 1.7, HB Chapter 8 p38}, the Sylow \(2\)-subgroups of \( G \) are order \( 4 \),
and the Sylow \(23\)-subgroups of \( G \) are order \( 23^{3} = 12167 \).

\vspace{2cm}

\qpart

Using The  Sylows Theorems, \textup{Theorem 1.12 , HB Chapter 8 p39},

For the Sylow \(2\)-subgroups of \( G \);

\[ n_2 \equiv 1 \pmod{2} \]
\[ n_{2} \mid 12167 \equiv 1 \pmod{2}  \]

given the factors of \( 12167 \) are \( 1, 23, 529, 12167 \), thus the possible values of \( n_{2} \) are;
\[ n_{2} = 1, 23, 529, 12167 \]
as all these possible alues are odd, they are all congruent to \( 1 \pmod{2} \).

For the Sylow \(23\)-subgroups of \( G \);
\[ n_{23} \mid 4 \equiv 1 \pmod{23}  \]

given the factors of \( 4 \) are \( 1, 2, 4 \), thus the possible value of \( n_{23} \) are;

\begin{align*}
    1 &\equiv 1 \pmod{23}\\
    2 &\equiv 2 \pmod{23}\\
    4 &\equiv 4 \pmod{23}
\end{align*}


as \( 1 \) is the only value congruent to \( 1 \pmod{23} \), thus by \textup{Lemma 1.9, HB Chapter 8 p39},
there is a unique Sylow \(23\)-subgroup of \( G \), which is normal.

\end{question}

\begin{question}
Question 9 from TMA02 material.

Let C[0,1] be the group of all continuous functions from [0,1] to \( \R \).
Let \( + \) be the operation defined By
\[ f + g \text{ is the function from } \left[0,1\right] \text{ to } \( \R \) \text{ given by } (f+g)(x)=f(x)+g(x) \]

\textbf{G1 Closure:}
If \( f,g \in C[0,1] \) then \( x \in C[0,1], f(x),g(x) \in \R \)

Since the sum of two continuous functions is continuous, \textup{Combination rules for continuous functions from \( \R \text{ to } \R \), HB Chapter 13 p65},
\[ f + g \in C[0,1]  \]

Hence, \( C[0,1] \) is closed under \( + \).

\vspace{2cm}

\textbf{G2 Identity:}
Let \( e \) be the function from \( [0,1] \text{ to } \R \) defined by \( e(x) = 0 \text{ for all } x \in [0,1] \).

Then for any \( f \in C[0,1] \) and \( x \in [0,1] \),
\begin{align*}
(f + e)(x) &= f(x) + e(x)\\
&= f(x) + 0\\
&= f(x)
\end{align*}

Thus, \( f + e = f \) for all \( f \in C[0,1] \), so \( e \) is the identity element of \( C[0,1] \).

\vspace{2cm}

\textbf{G3 Inverse:}
Define \( (-f)(x) = -f(x) \), for all \( x \in [0,1] \).

Since \( f \)  is continuous \( -f \) is also continuous, by \textup{Combination rules for continuous functions from \( \R \text{ to } \R \), HB Chapter 13 p65}.

Then 
\begin{align*}
(f+(-f))(x) &= f(x) - f(x) 
&= 0(x) 
&= e
\end{align*}

Thus every element has an inverse in \( C[0,1] \).

\vspace{2cm}

\textbf{G4 Associativity:}
Associativity holds because we are told in the question that  addition in \( \R \) is associative.

Hence, \( (C[0,1], +) \) is a group.
\end{question}
